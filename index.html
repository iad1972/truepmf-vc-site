If we called you

Before we invest, we call the buyers.

TruePMF Ventures is an investment firm. We do all the diligence other investors do. Then we do one thing most don't: we cold-call the people who would buy the product, and listen.

We exist to uncover companies with product-market fit before it's visible to other investors.

Product-market fit is evidence that a specific kind of buyer wants a specific product.

If we called you

A company we're looking at believes people like you want what it has built. We called to hear it from you.

Why your phone rang

We called about one specific company and its product. You were on the list on purpose: your role and your company match the buyer that company believes it has.

Who we are

We're an investment firm. Calling the people who would buy a product is part of how we decide whether to invest in the company behind it.

What happens next

If you were interested, the next step is a conversation with that company. If you weren't, that tells us something too. Either way, you owe us nothing.

To stop the calls

Email ilya@truepmf.vc and we won't call you again.

The usual diligence, and one thing more

Every pitch deck says the buyers are waiting. We call them.

  1. One belief.

    We start with the company's belief about who buys, and why. Before the first dial, we write down what evidence would change our minds.

  2. The exact list.

    We build the list of those exact people. If the list is wrong, nothing after it means anything.

  3. Live calls.

    A real pitch and a real ask, to strangers. No surveys, no role-play, no warm introductions that flatter the idea.

  4. A verdict.

    Keep, kill, revise, split, narrow or park. It goes into our decision alongside everything else we've learned.

The standard work tells us what a company says about its market. The calls tell us what the market says back.

On a call, a buyer can wave us off, recognize the problem, correct the pitch, or point us to the person who really owns it. Every one of those is evidence.

Why we work this way

A founder tells an investor the market wants the product. The investor has to decide whether to believe it.

Who in the room has the hard data? Nobody.

So we go and get it.

We think product-market fit shows up in what buyers say before it shows up in the numbers. By the time it's in the numbers, every investor can see it.

We'd rather hear it first.
